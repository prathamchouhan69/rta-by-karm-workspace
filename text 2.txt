CORRECTION: USE THE REFERENCES CREATIVELY — DO NOT REUSE THE OLD DESIGN

I do want you to use all supplied videos, images, and other creative references.

The restriction is against copying or recycling our OLD FRONTEND—not against studying the references.

This overrides any previous instruction telling you to ignore the supplied videos or images.

1. STUDY THE REFERENCES DEEPLY

Inspect the supplied videos and images in detail.

Study:
• Iconography.
• Motion graphics.
• Charts and graphs.
• Visual data representations.
• Navigation and sidebars.
• Transitions and micro-interactions.
• 3D geometry, depth, materials, and lighting.
• Camera movement.
• Animation timing, easing, choreography, and state changes.

Identify what makes each reference effective. Do not reduce the analysis to colours, screenshots, or general impressions.

Do not claim to have inspected a file or motion sequence that your tools cannot actually access.

2. INTERPRET THEM IN YOUR OWN UNIQUE, CREATIVE WAY

Use the references as creative input—not as templates to reproduce.

Extract their strongest principles, then transform, combine, and reimagine them into an original KARM experience.

For example:
• Study how a reference communicates navigation, then invent your own navigation composition and movement.
• Study how a chart animates relationships, then create an original visualization using the same underlying communication principle.
• Study an icon’s transformation, then develop your own icon geometry and transition.
• Study the relationship between camera, light, and objects, then create your own scene and choreography.

Do not reproduce exact scenes, layouts, assets, branding, or complete animation sequences.

The result must have its own identity—not look like a collage of the references.

3. DO NOT COPY OR RESKIN THE OLD FRONTEND

Build a genuinely new frontend design foundation.

Do not reuse the old:
• Screen compositions.
• Dashboard layout.
• Navigation or sidebar composition.
• Component styling.
• Chart presentation.
• Visual hierarchy.
• Motion system.

Changing colours, fonts, backgrounds, or border radii is not a redesign.

Preserve working functionality, data contracts, backend integrations, and business logic—but reinvent their visual and interactive expression.

“From scratch” means an original design foundation, not ignoring the references or unnecessarily rewriting working infrastructure.

4. APPLY THIS THROUGHOUT THE COMPLETE FRONTEND

Use your original interpretation of the references across:
• Navigation and sidebars.
• All screens and workflows.
• Iconography and components.
• Charts, graphs, maps, networks, and the complete visualization catalogue already specified.
• Loading, empty, error, success, and offline states.
• 3D scenes, transitions, and micro-interactions.

Where a visualization type is not shown in the references, extend your newly created design and motion language consistently.

5. KEEP THE THREE-MODE RULES

• Day: full creative freedom in colour and visual treatment.
• Night: full creative freedom in colour and visual treatment.
• Classic: based on our existing template theme, but not the old frontend layout.

Use the latest colour and typography instructions. Do not restore outdated green-only or old-palette restrictions.

Keep the existing KARM logo and required product identity.

6. CONTINUE IMPLEMENTATION

Continue building the complete frontend within the existing automation, testing, and safety boundaries.

Keep a concise record of:
• Which reference principle informed a decision.
• How you transformed it.
• What makes the resulting implementation original.

THE DIRECTION IS:

Use the supplied references deeply.
Do not copy them literally.
Do not recycle the old frontend.
Create your own unique, coherent, highly creative KARM design from what you learn.
